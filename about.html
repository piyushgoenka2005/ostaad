ABOUT OSTAAD

Construction has a coordination problem.

Building a home looks simple from the outside.

There is a blueprint. There is a contractor. There are materials. There is a budget.

But behind those four things is a much messier reality. Quantities sit in drawings. Prices sit with vendors. Specifications change over WhatsApp. Quotes arrive in different formats. Material decisions happen at different times.

And somewhere between the blueprint and the first purchase, the numbers start moving.

Ostaad exists to connect them.

The blueprint is not the budget.

A blueprint tells you what to build.

It doesn't automatically tell you:

  • How much material you need.
  • Which specification you are actually buying.
  • What the material should cost.
  • Whether two quotations are even comparable.

That gap is where much of construction's uncertainty begins.

THE REAL PROBLEM

Fragmentation.

A typical project can involve:

  • ARCHITECTDesigns the space.
  • ENGINEERDefines the technical requirements.
  • CONTRACTORBuilds the project.
  • VENDORQuotes the materials.
  • SUPPLIERMoves the materials.
  • HOMEOWNERUltimately pays for all of it.

Each person may have the information they need. But the project doesn't always have one connected version of the truth.

So the same house can have: One drawing. Three estimates. Ten vendor quotes. Different quantities. Different assumptions. And one very confused homeowner.

Eta-i problem.

The number on the quote isn't enough.

Someone says:

“Sir, construction cost ₹40 lakh.”

But what does that actually include?

Cement? Steel? Tiles? Electrical? Plumbing? Sanitaryware? Paint? Woodwork? Transport? Wastage?

What specification? What quantity? What rate?

Without that context, ₹40 lakh is not a cost plan. It's a number.

Ostaad is built to turn that number into something you can actually understand.

FROM BLUEPRINT TO COST

From blueprint to bill of quantities.

Construction professionals already have a language for this:

BOQ — BILL OF QUANTITIES

A BOQ connects project requirements with measurable quantities, rates and amounts. It is one of the fundamental ways construction work is translated into a costable scope.

The problem is that for many homeowners, this information remains buried inside spreadsheets, contractor estimates, drawings and individual vendor conversations.

Ostaad brings that logic into a simpler experience.

WHY STANDARDISATION MATTERS

Same requirement. Clearer comparison.

Imagine two vendors quote for the same project.

  • VENDOR A₹8.2 lakh
  • VENDOR B₹7.6 lakh

Which one is cheaper? You can't know until you know:

Are they quoting the same quantity? The same specification? The same material? The same scope?

A lower number isn't automatically a better deal.

Ostaad creates a common costing reference so comparisons become more meaningful.

India doesn't have a material problem. It has an information problem.

India has an enormous construction-material ecosystem. But the market remains highly fragmented, particularly across suppliers and intermediaries.

That means finding a material isn't necessarily the hard part. The hard part is knowing:

  • What should I buy?
  • How much should I buy?
  • What specification do I need?
  • What should it cost?
  • Who should I buy it from?
  • When should it arrive?

Ostaad is being built around that information layer.
